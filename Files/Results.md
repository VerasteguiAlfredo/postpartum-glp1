# Results

## Cohort Characteristics

Among postpartum women screened for first-time GLP-1 RA exposure within 12 months following delivery, 704 patients constituted the final analytic cohort (Figure 1). Of these, 297 (42.2%) initiated GLP-1 RA therapy within 6 months of delivery (Early group) and 407 (57.8%) at 6 months or later (Late group). Outcome-specific analytic subsets comprised 652 patients with documented baseline weight (weight outcomes), 633 patients with documented pre-pregnancy weight (return-to-pre-pregnancy outcome), and 440 patients meeting hypertensive disorder of pregnancy (HDP) criteria (blood pressure outcomes). After exclusion for missing covariate data, Cox models were fit on 611, 577, and 366 patients, respectively.

Baseline characteristics of the overall cohort and by GLP-1 initiation timing are presented in Table 1. The median maternal age at delivery was 33.0 years (IQR 29.0–36.0), median baseline BMI was 39.6 kg/m² (IQR 35.2–44.8), and median baseline blood pressure was 121/79 mmHg. Class III obesity (BMI ≥40 kg/m²) was present in 310 patients (48.0%), and Stage 1 or higher hypertension at baseline in 334 (52.0%). Hypertensive disorders complicated 430 pregnancies (61.1%), preeclampsia spectrum 201 (28.6%), and gestational diabetes 228 (32.4%).

The Early and Late initiation groups were similar in age, race, ethnicity, baseline BMI, baseline blood pressure, parity, gravidity, delivery mode, and choice of GLP-1 agent (all p > 0.05; Table 1). By design, the median interval from delivery to GLP-1 initiation differed (111 days [IQR 73–147] vs 270 days [IQR 230–313]; p < 0.001). Statistically significant between-group differences were observed for documented obesity (86% Early vs 78% Late; p = 0.006), type 2 diabetes (21% vs 13%; p = 0.009), obstructive sleep apnea (9.8% vs 4.7%; p = 0.010), current smoking (14% vs 8.6%; p = 0.017), and baseline HbA1c (median 5.2% vs 5.5%; p < 0.001). GLP-1 persistence categories differed between groups (p < 0.001), with 21% of Early initiators reaching ≥12 months of continuous therapy versus 7.9% of Late initiators.

## GLP-1 Therapy Characteristics

The most commonly prescribed first postpartum GLP-1 RA was semaglutide (325 patients, 46%), followed by tirzepatide (299, 42%), liraglutide (56, 8.0%), dulaglutide (21, 3.0%), and exenatide (2, 0.3%) (Table 2). Weight-loss-indicated formulations were prescribed for 525 patients (75%) and type 2 diabetes formulations for 177 (25%). The median GLP-1 prescription duration was 151 days (IQR 66–262), and 167 patients (24%) received more than one GLP-1 agent during follow-up. Active GLP-1 prescriptions were documented for 236 patients (34%) at 6 months postpartum and 423 (60%) at 12 months postpartum. The distribution of GLP-1 agents, branded indication, prescription duration, and persistence did not differ significantly when stratified by baseline blood pressure status (Tables 2a–2c).

## Weight Outcomes

Time-to-event results for the three weight outcomes are presented in Table 3 and Figure 2. Within the weight-outcome cohort (n = 611), 98 patients (16.0%) achieved ≥10% weight loss within 18 months of GLP-1 initiation, at a median of 7.8 months overall (Early 8.3 months vs Late 5.2 months; log-rank p = 0.084). Twenty-two patients (3.6%) achieved ≥20% weight loss; median time-to-event was not reached in either group (log-rank p = 0.968). In the pre-pregnancy weight subset (n = 577), 250 patients (43.3%) returned to or below their pre-pregnancy weight at a median of 3.9 months overall (Early 4.4 months vs Late 3.2 months; log-rank p = 0.099).

Adjusted hazard ratios (Late vs Early initiation) were 1.60 (95% CI 0.90–2.85; p = 0.112) for ≥10% weight loss, 1.23 (95% CI 0.12–12.80; p = 0.865) for ≥20% weight loss, and 1.22 (95% CI 0.93–1.62; p = 0.156) for return to pre-pregnancy weight (Table 3).

## Blood Pressure Outcomes

Time-to-event results for the three blood pressure outcomes in the HDP subgroup (n = 366) are presented in Table 4 and Figure 3. The ≥5 mmHg SBP decline endpoint was reached by 172 patients (47.0%) at a median of 2.9 months overall (Early 3.2 months vs Late 2.5 months; log-rank p = 0.055). The ≥10 mmHg SBP decline endpoint was reached by 138 patients (37.7%) at a median of 4.3 months (Early 4.6 months vs Late 3.3 months; log-rank p = 0.148). The ≥5 mmHg DBP decline endpoint was reached by 157 patients (42.9%) at a median of 3.3 months (Early 3.9 months vs Late 2.5 months; log-rank p = 0.008).

Adjusted hazard ratios (Late vs Early initiation) were 1.37 (95% CI 0.98–1.93; p = 0.065) for ≥5 mmHg SBP decline, 1.35 (95% CI 0.92–1.97; p = 0.122) for ≥10 mmHg SBP decline, and 1.59 (95% CI 1.11–2.27; p = 0.011) for ≥5 mmHg DBP decline (Table 4).

## Summary of Effect Estimates

Adjusted hazard ratios for all six outcomes are summarized in Figure 4. All point estimates exceeded 1.0, ranging from 1.22 to 1.60. The ≥5 mmHg DBP decline endpoint was the only outcome reaching statistical significance at the conventional α = 0.05 threshold (adjusted p = 0.011).

## Patient-Level Timelines

Patient-level timelines for the ≥10% weight loss outcome are displayed in Figure 5, plotting all 98 patients who reached the endpoint and a random sample of 40 censored patients (Early n = 97; Late n = 41). Among patients who reached the endpoint, the median time on drug to event was 4.4 months (IQR 3.2–5.9); among censored patients, the median on-drug follow-up was 1.9 months (IQR 0.0–4.1).
